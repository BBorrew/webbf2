About

I study geometry that looks right but is wrong. Feed-forward 3D models produce plausible, self-consistent reconstructions at mirrors and glass that no camera could have seen; sparse-view generative models must represent what was measured and what was not; motion blur hides when, not only where, a surface was visible. My work builds counterfactual benchmarks and models that make these failures identifiable and correctable.

I am in the final year of the five-year integrated master's (MInf) in Informatics at the University of Edinburgh, after an exchange year at the University of California, Davis. I am applying to PhD programs starting in autumn 2027.

Question

Consistent but wrong

Mirrors and glass pass every multi-view check and still give the wrong geometry.

Method

Counterfactual capture

Paired scenes that differ in one physical factor, so the failure is measurable.

Stance

Models that know their limits

Reconstruction that flags what the images cannot identify.

Manuscripts in preparation

Beyond Straight Rays: Counterfactual Auditing of Feed-Forward 3D Geometry at Mirrors and GlassIn preparation

A paired-capture dataset of mirror and glass planes, an audit showing that validity masks accept reflected content as geometry, a single-frame stress test for global models, and a light-path pointmap model that predicts path type and physical endpoint.

OneScene: Observation-Adaptive Scene Spaces for Joint Geometry and AppearanceIn preparation

A generative sparse-view reconstruction model whose continuous function space adapts to the observations before any mesh or image is requested.

Shutter Transport: Correcting Visibility in Switch-Time SpaceIn preparation

Motion blur confounds radiance with the times at which surfaces become visible; switch-time correction separates the two and improves deblurring.
